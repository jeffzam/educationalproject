# Classroom guide

## Forces and Motion · 40 minutes

**Intention** — Predict how friction affects motion, then explain the outcome using a fair comparison. Curriculum link: Year 5 Primary Science 5.1.3.

- 8 minutes. Ask pupils how a toy moves on a tiled floor and on a rug. Record a prediction with a reason.
- 22 minutes. Release the cart with Smoother surface selected, switch to Rougher surface and release it again. Ask students to use the comparison to verify friction was the only setting that changed before they interpret the distances.
- 10 minutes. Write an explanation that connects friction and distance. Reflect on an assumption the model makes.
- Follow-up. Test a new surface using a real toy and agree what to keep the same.

## Space and Earth · 30 minutes

**Intention** — Use a model to explain day and night. Curriculum link: Year 5 Primary Science 5.9.1.

- Set local solar time to noon. Ask pupils to predict where Malta will be relative to the lit half of Earth at midnight.
- Record noon and midnight while holding the orbital day and tilt fixed. Use both observations to explain the role of Earth’s rotation.
- For an extension, switch to Earth’s orbit and compare daylight around the June and December solstices. Change the lunar day separately and describe what changes in the Moon inset.
- Compare the Moon inset with observations or with [NASA’s Moon Phases guide](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/).

## Roman Malta · 40 minutes

**Intention** — Draw and qualify an inference about a Roman household. Curriculum link: Year 7 History General, HIS 7.4, objective 3, item 9.

- Inspect the mosaic, courtyard and nearby remains. Identify which features survive and which are visual interpretations.
- Ask the fictional artisan a question. Discuss whether a rich household represents other households.
- Write a claim about domestic life. Include one source that supports it and one thing the source cannot establish.
- Follow-up. Compare the inference with a different archaeological site or artefact.

## Save a Maltese Coastal Town · 40 minutes

**Intention** — Describe flooding impacts and use a comparison to justify an intervention. Curriculum link: Year 8 Geography General 8.3.

- Read the national risk assessment entry for 15 September 2003. Identify the flooding process and document two effects.
- Record the fictional coastal baseline. Compare two interventions within a €1 million budget and note costs, exposed locations and potential impacts.
- Explain the strongest option and why the real rainfall case and fictional coastal scenario require different kinds of evidence.
- Follow-up. Identify what a planner would need to know about drainage, elevation or maintenance before recommending an actual project.

## Formative rubric

Apply each criterion separately. Choose Support, Core or Challenge as scaffolding for the activity and revisit it as needed.

| Criterion | Emerging | Developing | Secure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual understanding | Names a relevant idea | Describes it accurately in context | Uses it to explain the observed relationship |
| Reasoning | States a conclusion | Connects the conclusion to a result | Explains why the link follows and considers a limitation |
| Evidence | Names a trial or source | Selects clear relevant evidence | Compares evidence and identifies what would strengthen it |

Use the original outcome and the complete submission when deciding what has been demonstrated. These rubric descriptors are proposed teaching criteria.
